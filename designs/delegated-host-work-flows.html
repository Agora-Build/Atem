Delegated host work and host senders: how data, commands and authority flow

Six diagrams of the paths between Auth, the Portal and its admin site, Astation (the native app and its Station server) and Atem hosts. Read them next to delegated-host-work-and-host-senders.md (revision 5), whose Appendix A holds the owner's decisions of 2026-11-07 word for word.

What this page follows from the owner's decisions
  • Host senders: Revoke + Readmit only. No hold or release and no principal_seq for host: senders. Integration Pause / Resume stays as it is.
  • Re-verification never restores sending. A revoke survives every key change of the same host_id; only an explicit Readmit lifts it.
  • Two authorities. R9 authorization belongs to native managers. Agora.Build decides only whether it keeps serving its own relay and mailbox, and that is never an R9 revocation.
  • Disabled account, option B. The built-in Station refuses relay and mailbox service to that account's hosts. Proposed: Auth/Portal and Astation define it jointly.
  • Deferred device-list entries, option A. During an account recovery every unstarted delegated remote task waits; running and local work go on.
Status

Nothing here is built. The core model is approved in principle, and the choices marked decided are the owner's. Everything still needs review and agreement from Atem, Astation and Auth/Portal before it is implemented, merged or deployed.

Marked proposed: designs the decisions asked for but nobody has agreed yet, such as the exact R9 form of Revoke and Readmit, and the disabled-account binding. Every owner choice is now decided (choice 19 was withdrawn).

01

Who talks to whom

Every remote path goes through a Station, but the Station only routes, stores and relays. What gives a message authority is a key the receiving host already pins, not the path it took.

Reading it

  • Stations. The built-in Station is station.agora.build, the dev one station-dev.agora.build. A custom Station is run by someone else and serves the same roles.
  • One link per host. A host opens no port and never connects to another host. Channels, control_sync, its own device-list fetch and the mailbox calls all ride its one outbound relay link (or Astation's local :8080 proxy), authenticated by the paired Astation or by its host route proof.
  • The Station holds keys only for integrations. It signs their stops with their own R9 service keys. For native members and host senders it holds no key a host trusts.
  • Two sign-ins per device. The Mac and the phone each reach the Station's relay with an astation.remote token for the selected Station, while each atem-ch1 channel to a host is authenticated by the device's own key against the device list. The phone's login is live on dev (Auth #19, merged as c494bae; updated after agreement). Redirects: build.agora.astation.ios:/oauth/callback and build.agora.astation.android:/oauth/callback.
  • Native devices carry entries too. Inside their channels they hand hosts newer device-list entries, so a Station that withholds them can't hide them from a host a native device reaches.
  • Hosts trust signatures, not carriers. Every entry, record and envelope is verified with the key the host's own device list names.

What each side can't do

  • The relay can't read channels or tell who is inside them.
  • The mailbox can't read, forge or alter an envelope. It can delay, withhold or drop one, within its 24 h life, the senders' resends and the gate.
  • Auth, the Portal and the admin site hold no key that can sign a native R9 entry or a host sender's revoke.

02

Authority layers

The owner's model: user authentication, command verification and a safe transport channel. Prompts come from the authenticated user, with no fake one and no one in the middle. On top of that sit two separate authorities.

Reading it

  • Authentication, twice for each native device. The Mac and the phone each have an Agora.Build login (Auth tokens: service access to the Station; the phone's is live on dev since 2026-11-08, Auth #19, updated after agreement; fig. 1) and an R9 native membership (their device keys in the device list, a log signed by native managers: authority over hosts). A disabled Agora.Build account stops the phone reaching the built-in Station, because Auth refuses its tokens, and changes nothing in R9.
  • Channel principal. The atem-ch1 handshake binds each channel to a member and the key its device-list entry pins.
  • Admission record. At enqueue the host freezes who asked (principal and key hash), the route revision, the grant, the payload digest and a deadline at most 24 h ahead. Nothing is fetched from the sender later.
  • Start check. Run in the same step that marks the attempt executing, so a task either started and runs, or never ran.
  • Transport. A Station can delay or withhold traffic, but each task's deadline, the revocation gate and the senders' resends bound what that can do.

The two authorities decided

R9 authorization decides whether a host is trusted and allowed to send work: native managers only. Agora.Build gets no key that could forge a revocation or change a project grant.

Service availability belongs to each Station's operator: Agora.Build for the built-in Station, the owner of a custom one. Refusing service writes no record and no device-list entry, changes no R9 state, and is shown as a service suspension, never as a revocation.

Integrations are managed in the Portal (and the owner can revoke one natively): their stops are signed with the integration's own key, which the Station holds, and reach only that integration's work.

03

An integration's task, start to end

Once host B durably accepts the task, Atem owns it. The integration may go offline or expire; only a revocation, a narrowed grant or a route change stops unstarted work. decided

Reading 3a

  • Steps 1–5: admission. Checked once, against the sender's authority at that moment, and frozen into the task. The reply in step 5 comes only after the record is durable.
  • Step 7: the revocation gate. It closes on a disconnect, a feed reset, 60 s without a head, and at every restart. While it is closed, unstarted remote tasks wait with revocations not yet checked; local tasks and running turns go on.
  • Account recovery. While a device-list entry is deferred, every unstarted delegated remote task waits with device list update pending, then is checked again option A.
  • Step 8: the start check. Revocation, grant, route, device-list state and deadline are read again in the step that marks executing.
  • Steps 9–11. The prompt runs under the host-local host-work principal, not on the sender's channel; the receipt records the task and who asked.
  • Steps 12–15. Queries need the sender's current authority. Expiry blocks them; a renewal makes the reply readable again.

Reading 3b

  • Three ways in. The owner's revoke in the Portal is recorded by the Portal and appended to its Station feed directly; Auth's change feed doesn't drive it. A native revoke is also signed with the manager's key and appended to the feed, and is mirrored to the Portal the same way. An admin's revoke goes to Auth with a reason and a step-up; Auth's unsigned nudge makes the Portal read Auth's change feed.
  • The poll is the authority. The Portal's push to the Station is only a hint. The Station pulls GET /api/v1/stations/changes with its station.report token, then signs its own revoke with the integration's service key, which stays in force even if a native signer is removed later.
  • Unstarted tasks are cancelled as soon as host B applies the record.
  • Running turns continue unless the owner chose to interrupt. The Portal also offers "interrupt running tasks" later, within 24 h of the revocation.
  • Only an applied result counts. The Station sends revocation_applied{host, record_id} only for a host's per-record result applied, never for its feed cursor: a host can scan past a record it skipped or holds pending. duplicate, pending and refused stay between the host and the Station and never reach the Portal.
  • Revoking → Revoked. The final report is revoked{outcome: "all_applied" | "deadline_passed", pending_hosts}; the deadline is 24 h, when every task has passed its own. The Portal shows "Revoked" for all_applied and "Revoked (n hosts didn't confirm…)" for deadline_passed.
  • Pause / Resume of integrations is unchanged: hold and release records, signed the same way.
04

A host-to-host message

An agent on host A asks an agent on host B to do something. A host never opens a channel to another host: it sends a signed, sealed envelope through the Station's mailbox, and the result comes back the same way, possibly long after A went offline.

Reading it

  • Steps 1–3: publish before notify. A's outbox publishes the context to memory or the vault, reads it back, and freezes the exact snapshots into the message. B never fetches anything from A later.
  • The envelope. Signed with A's newest host_key and sealed (HPKE) to B's host_enc_key, a separate signed device-list entry. The Station sees only the account, both hosts, ids, times and size.
  • Steps 7–10: B's checks. Two locks must both be open: a signed project that lists A and B with the ops, and B's own route consent (project:<name> or host:<id> in runtime.toml). Labels address agents; they never authorize.
  • Steps 13–15. The task runs on B like any delegated task: gate, start check at executing, framed prompt, with A offline or not.
  • Steps 17–20: two outcomes. From an authorized B, A delivers the result into the asking session once, framed as data, on a route with results = true. From a B that was revoked, removed or is no longer in a project with A, the result is stored as data on A, shown by atem message status and to the owner, and never delivered into a session.

The authorization check proposed

Each envelope and result names signed_at_head: the seq and hash of the sender's device-list head when it signed. B accepts it only if A is authorized(g), the seq is at least g, and the hash matches B's own entry there. So nothing signed before a Readmit is accepted after it, and a revoked host can't pre-sign for a head that doesn't exist yet. Atem's recommendation; it needs agreement.

Limits that stay

  • No attachments or marks from host senders in this version.
  • Mailbox calls up to 512 KiB; a Message keeps its 240 KiB bound.
  • A host sender can't query, interrupt, resolve or answer approvals; its cancel and complete are envelopes too.
05

Host-sender authorization: Revoke and Readmit

Two states and two moves, both made only by a native manager. Keys and project membership are kept apart from this state. decided representation proposed

Reading it

  • Revoke (host_sender_revoke) blocks the host's current authorization for good: new envelopes are refused, unstarted tasks are cancelled once each receiver applies it, and running turns are interrupted only if the owner chose interrupt_running, then or later with a native interrupt record.
  • Readmit (host_sender_readmit) is a separate, explicit act a native manager confirms. Its seq becomes the new generation: only work signed after it is accepted. It never revives cancelled tasks, never replays old envelopes, and both entries stay in the log.
  • Keys are not permission. atem pair verify, a new add_host key, a host_enc_key rotation, even remove_host then add_host of the same host_id: none of them changes this state, which is keyed by host_id. A key change also no longer cancels tasks already admitted; the owner revokes to stop them.
  • Projects are a separate lock. project_set and project_remove decide which hosts may talk and with which ops. remove_host ends every membership; a host added back starts a new membership epoch, so its old tasks can't start, and it needs a new project_set (and a Readmit, if it was revoked).
  • Nobody else decides. A host's own key never signs its own state, and Auth, the Portal and the admin site can't revoke or readmit a host sender.

Gone since revision 1

  • Native host-sender hold and release, and Pause / Resume for host senders in Astation.
  • The principal_seq counter, its synchronization and sender_state.
  • "Re-verification lifts a revoke": replaced by Readmit.

Unchanged

  • Integration Pause / Resume and Revoke (fig. 3b).
06

A disabled Agora.Build account

Option B: the built-in Station stops serving relay and mailbox to that account's hosts. A service restriction only: R9 trust, keys and grants don't change. proposed

Reading it

  • Release R9 binding D27, not built yet goes through the Portal, not Auth's feed. The Station reports bindings to the Portal with its station.report token: binding_changed{account, account_id, genesis_hash, binding_id, version, state}. The admin site reads them and releases one through the Portal's admin API, with a portal.admin token that Auth exchanges from the admin's step-up proof; Auth's part is that authority and the audit. The Portal sends binding_released{account, binding_id, version} in its feed to the built-in Station, which refuses a stale or repeated one and changes the binding and the room ownership together, as unbind and transfer do. None of it changes R9, and it gives the admin site no new trust path.
  • Disable. Auth writes account_disabled in its change feed, ahead of the account's per-integration rows. The Portal's Station feed carries it to the built-in Station only.
  • Which hosts. The relay, bound or not: one durable service-owner lookup for every built-in transport being built by Astation. Registered identity rooms resolve through the verified issuer/subject join, proved R9 routes through the durable recovery-root/host association, and session rooms are bound before they become persistent routes. Unknown ownership is never treated as enabled. The new stage-3 mailbox and host-sender feeds are served only to R9 accounts bound by a proof.
  • The Station refuses and closes host relay links (routed too), channels routed to them, mailbox calls, control_sync, the device-list fetch and push relay messages. Stored mail stays, undelivered, until it expires. Integrations of the account are held, as already designed.
  • The close code needs agreement is service_suspended, text "service suspended: account disabled at Agora.Build": no admin, no reason, never "revoked". A route proof gets host_route_refused{code: "service_suspended"}, an open host link a service_suspended relay message before it closes, and channels to the host close{reason: "service_suspended"}, so native devices see the same reason.
  • The hosts keep running turns. Unstarted delegated remote tasks wait behind the closed gate, since no feed or mailbox can be synced; local tasks run. Messages not yet accepted by a receiving host may never be delivered and expire within 24 h. atem status shows the text on its Relay line; the host keeps retrying at its normal pace and treats it as an outage, never a revocation: no R9 state changes and nothing is fenced.
  • Enable. Service resumes only after the hosts authenticate, reconnect and fully sync the feed, the device list and the mailbox. Expiry, ids and signed_at_head keep anything stale from replaying, and every revocation still applies.
  • Restarts. The Station stores the enforcement state durably and applies it before it serves anything after a restart.
  • Custom Stations get no account-level events in this version and keep their own policy.

The binding proposed

Agora.Build account ↔ R9 account_id + genesis_hash, created only with an astation.remote sign-in and a native manager's signature, with a fresh confirmation, over a Station challenge that names the device-list head.

  • A second account trying to bind is refused; the bound owner is alerted.
  • A transfer needs a fresh proof and both accounts; the admin site can release a binding (above). Unbind, release and transfer move the binding and the room ownership together.
  • A re-pair after a freeze is a new device list and needs a new binding.
  • Owner choice 19 withdrawn (a forced binding after a migration window): room ownership already covers the relay. It returns only if Astation finds a host link or routed channel the Station can't map to a room, and then only for those links.

Disabling gives Agora.Build no way to read host messages or forge native R9 records.
